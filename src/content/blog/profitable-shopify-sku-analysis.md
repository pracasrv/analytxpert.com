# How to Do a Profitable SKU Analysis (And Kill the Losers)

Here's an uncomfortable truth: **Some of your best-selling products are losing you money.**

They generate revenue. They have decent ROAS. But after factoring in COGS, returns, and ad spend allocation, they're bleeding profit.

## Step 1: Calculate Contribution Margin by SKU

For each product, calculate:

```
Revenue
- COGS
- Shipping
- Payment processing fees
- Allocated ad spend
- Returns/refunds
= Contribution Margin
```

### Example

**Product A:**
- Selling price: $50, COGS: $18, Shipping: $6, Fees: $1.50, Ad spend/unit: $12, Return rate: 15%
- **Contribution Margin: $5** ✅

**Product B:**
- Selling price: $80, COGS: $35, Shipping: $9, Fees: $2.40, Ad spend/unit: $20, Return rate: 25%
- **Contribution Margin: -$6.40** ❌

## Step 2: Identify the Losers

Look for SKUs with:
- **Negative contribution margin**
- **High return rates** (>20%)
- **Low repeat purchase rates** (<10%)

## Step 3: Decide What to Do

### Option 1: Kill It
Stop advertising it. Remove it from your store.

**Best for:** Products with structural problems (poor quality, bad fit).

### Option 2: Fix the Economics
- Raise the price
- Lower COGS (negotiate with supplier)
- Reduce ad spend

### Option 3: Bundle It
Pair the unprofitable SKU with a high-margin product.

### Option 4: Use It as a Loss Leader
Accept the loss if it drives repeat purchases of profitable products.

## Real Example

**Client:** Home goods brand, $600k/month revenue

**Problem:** Best-selling product (30% of revenue) had -8% contribution margin.

**What I did:**
1. Analyzed return reasons → Most due to "smaller than expected"
2. Updated product images and description
3. Raised price by $5
4. Paused bottom 20% of ad spend

**Result:** Return rate dropped from 22% to 14%. Contribution margin went from -8% to +12%. Profit increased by $18k/month.

## The Bottom Line

Revenue isn't profit. Some SKUs need to be killed, not scaled.
