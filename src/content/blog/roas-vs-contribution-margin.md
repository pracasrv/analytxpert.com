# Why ROAS is Lying to You (And What to Track Instead)

If you're running a Shopify store spending $50k+ on ads per month, you've probably heard this before: "Just get your ROAS above 3x and you're profitable."

**That's wrong.**

## The Problem With ROAS

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) only tells you one thing: revenue generated per dollar spent on ads.

What it **doesn't** tell you:
- Cost of goods sold (COGS)
- Shipping costs
- Returns and refunds
- Payment processing fees
- Platform fees
- Attribution errors

## Real Example

One of my clients had a "Scale" campaign showing 4.0x ROAS on Meta. Looks great, right?

After I calculated the **true contribution margin**, I found:
- Product cost: $28
- Shipping: $8
- Average return rate on this campaign: 18%
- True profit per sale: **-$4.12**

They were scaling losses at 4x ROAS.

## What to Track Instead: Contribution Margin

Contribution margin = Revenue - ALL variable costs (including ad spend).

This tells you: **"After I pay for the product, shipping, ads, and fees, how much is left?"**

### How to Calculate It

```
Revenue from campaign: $100
- COGS: $25
- Shipping: $7
- Ad spend: $30
- Fees (3%): $3
= Contribution Margin: $35
```

If that number is positive, you can scale. If it's negative, you're losing money—no matter what ROAS says.

## The Fix

Start tracking contribution margin by:
1. Campaign
2. Product/SKU
3. Channel
4. Creative

You'll instantly see which ads are making money and which are just generating vanity metrics.
