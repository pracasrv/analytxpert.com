# How to Calculate True Contribution Margin for Your Shopify Store

Contribution margin is the single most important metric for knowing if your Shopify store is actually profitable.

Here's how to calculate it correctly.

## The Formula

```
Contribution Margin = Revenue - Variable Costs
```

### Variable Costs Include:
- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
- Shipping costs
- Payment processing fees (usually 2.9% + $0.30)
- Ad spend
- Returns and refunds
- Transaction fees (Shopify fees)

## Step-by-Step Example

Let's say you sell a product for **$100**.

### Revenue
- Sale price: $100

### Variable Costs
- COGS: $30
- Shipping: $8
- Payment processing (3%): $3
- Ad spend to acquire this sale: $25
- Shopify transaction fee (0.5%): $0.50
- Returns (10% rate, so $10 expected cost): $10

**Total Variable Costs: $76.50**

### Contribution Margin
```
$100 - $76.50 = $23.50
```

**Contribution Margin %:** 23.5%

## What This Means

For every $100 sale, you have $23.50 left over to cover fixed costs and profit.

If your contribution margin is **negative**, you're losing money on every sale—no matter how much revenue you generate.

## Why ROAS Doesn't Show This

If you spent $25 on ads to make a $100 sale, your ROAS is 4.0x.

But after all costs, you only kept $23.50. ROAS didn't account for COGS, shipping, fees, or returns.

## How to Track This

Calculate contribution margin by:
1. **Campaign** (which ads are profitable?)
2. **Product/SKU** (which products make money?)
3. **Channel** (Meta vs Google vs TikTok)
4. **Customer cohort** (new vs returning)
